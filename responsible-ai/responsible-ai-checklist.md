# Responsible AI Checklist for Creative Work

A lightweight review framework for AI-assisted design and creative
production.

**PROTECT → VERIFY → OWN**

Responsible AI does not need to mean adding a heavy approval layer to
every creative task.

The goal is to create a few reliable habits that allow teams to use AI
confidently while keeping human judgment, creative ownership, and
accountability at the center.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. PROTECT

Be intentional about what information enters the AI workflow.

### Before using AI

-   [ ] Am I using an appropriate, approved AI environment?
-   [ ] Do I understand what source material the task actually requires?
-   [ ] Am I providing only the information needed for the task?
-   [ ] Have I removed unnecessary confidential, proprietary, personal,
    or sensitive details where appropriate?
-   [ ] Have I preserved required classification or handling
    information?
-   [ ] Have I checked files for hidden or unnecessary context before
    sharing them?

### Principle

**Provide what the task needs---not everything available.**

A sanitized working copy can reduce unnecessary exposure, but changing a
file format does not automatically make information safe to share.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. VERIFY

Treat AI output as a draft or contribution that requires human review.

### Review for

-   [ ] factual accuracy;
-   [ ] completeness;
-   [ ] correct interpretation of requirements;
-   [ ] visual quality;
-   [ ] brand alignment;
-   [ ] accessibility;
-   [ ] usability;
-   [ ] appropriate tone;
-   [ ] unintended changes;
-   [ ] unsupported assumptions.

AI-generated output can appear polished while still being incorrect or
incomplete.

### For revisions

Also verify:

-   [ ] the requested change is present;
-   [ ] protected elements remain unchanged;
-   [ ] existing functionality still works;
-   [ ] interaction states remain correct;
-   [ ] responsive behavior remains intact where applicable.

### Principle

**Polished is not the same as verified.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. OWN

Human teams remain responsible for the final work.

AI can assist with:

-   organizing;
-   drafting;
-   exploring;
-   transforming;
-   prototyping;
-   implementing;
-   documenting;
-   identifying possibilities.

People remain responsible for:

-   defining the problem;
-   deciding what information is appropriate to use;
-   choosing the creative direction;
-   resolving ambiguity;
-   evaluating quality;
-   approving the result;
-   understanding what ships.

### Principle

**AI can contribute to the process. People own the outcome.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Quick Pre-Ship Check

Before AI-assisted creative work is considered complete, ask:

### INPUT

**Did we use an appropriate tool and provide only appropriate source
material?**

### BRIEF

**Did we give the AI enough context, constraints, references, and
preservation requirements?**

### REVIEW

**Has a person reviewed the result for accuracy, quality, accessibility,
usability, and brand fit?**

### PROTECT

**If this was a revision, did we preserve approved functionality and
avoid unrelated changes?**

### REUSE

**Did this project reveal a useful pattern worth carrying forward?**

This should be a quick mental checklist---not unnecessary bureaucracy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Responsible Acceleration

A useful shorthand is:

**Approved environment + Appropriate inputs + Human review = Responsible
acceleration**

AI can increase speed.

Responsibility helps prevent that speed from becoming unnecessary risk,
confusion, or rework.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Capturing Reusable Learning

After a successful project, consider whether anything should become:

-   a reusable template;
-   a component;
-   a prompt pattern;
-   a checklist;
-   a workflow;
-   a documented standard;
-   an approved reference.

Do not preserve everything.

Preserve what will materially improve future work.
